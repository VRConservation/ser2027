**Title**: Rabbits, wildcats, pond turtles and a river that stayed: one open connectivity screen for trophic and hydrological rewilding in the Greater Côa Valley, Portugal

**Format**: Poster. **Series**: Lands in Motion.

**Author**: Linda Angulo Lopez, Côa Connectivity Lab. ORCID 0009-0009-5689-7148.

## Abstract

Rewilding projects usually treat predators, prey and rivers as three separate questions. In the Greater Côa Valley, northern Portugal, they meet in three animals: the European rabbit, keystone prey of the Iberian Peninsula and Endangered in its native range; the European wildcat, which depends on it; and the pond turtle, which holds on where summer pools hold water. We ran one screen for all three on an open, laptop-scale connectivity workflow (10,230 km², ten focal species, circuit theory in pure Python, with penalties for recent fire, 22 field-recorded barriers and two UNESCO World Heritage areas). Rabbit release candidates were limited to scrub-pasture mosaic inside the top third of the wildcat's land corridors. This gave 1,045 km² and ten priority survey blocks, 87% of it on hunting land. The pond turtle screen found 96 pools with water all year, 48 of them on better-connected rivers, 13 inside the Côa rock-art core and only one within 2 km of a rabbit area. The wildcat is supported, not released, through neutering of domestic cats and mesopredator monitoring. The Côa is free-flowing because a 1995 civic campaign, "As gravuras não sabem nadar", stopped a dam to save its rock art. We pair the maps with new drawings of the three animals in the valley's Iron Age fine-line style. We propose (i) a rabbit baseline run with hunters, (ii) an August pool survey before any turtle release, and (iii) adding both species to the workflow to test whether the rock-art corridor stays the best combined corridor. Which animals will the next chapter of the Côa include, and who decides?

**Keywords**: rewilding; European rabbit; European wildcat; pond turtle; ecological connectivity; circuit theory; free-flowing rivers; World Heritage; open science; Portugal
